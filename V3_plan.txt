BazarSetu
AI/ML Systems Blueprint v1.0
Document type: Product, AI/ML and Technical Architecture Specification
Project: BazarSetu
Architecture: Hybrid AI Architecture
Status: Planning and specification
Scope: Marketplace product images and product intelligence
Primary objective: Build a reliable AI-powered system that transforms ordinary seller product photographs and voice descriptions into accurate, professional, marketplace-ready product listings.

1. Project Vision and Scope
BazarSetu aims to simplify digital commerce for Indian artisans, small manufacturers, local businesses and retailers.
Many sellers do not have professional photography equipment, product photography knowledge, English writing skills or experience creating structured e-commerce catalogues.
BazarSetu will address these problems through an integrated AI/ML platform.
A seller should be able to:
Capture photographs of a product using a smartphone.
Record a description in their preferred language.
Receive professionally enhanced marketplace images.
Automatically identify product details.
Generate a structured product catalogue.
Receive pricing assistance.
Review and approve the generated information.
Publish an accurate product listing.
Architecture philosophy
BazarSetu will own its AI orchestration, data, processing pipelines, product-specific intelligence, validation and evaluation systems.
It will combine:
Open-source pretrained models.
External AI APIs.
Custom machine-learning models where justified.
Deterministic image-processing algorithms.
BazarSetu-specific business rules and intelligence.
The system must remain independent of any single AI provider.
Scope boundaries
Included:
Product image quality assessment.
Product detection and segmentation.
Marketplace image enhancement.
Product visual understanding.
Voice intelligence.
Multimodal intelligence.
Product catalogue intelligence.
Pricing intelligence.
Product verification.
Model management, evaluation and infrastructure.
Excluded from this version:
Marketing images.
Promotional posters.
Advertisement creatives.
Lifestyle product photography.
Social media image generation.
AI-generated product redesign.
The focus is exclusively on trustworthy marketplace product images and product intelligence.

SYSTEM 01: PRODUCT IMAGE INTELLIGENCE
1.1 Why do we need to build this system?
Most small sellers and artisans capture product photographs using ordinary smartphones.
Their photographs may contain:
Cluttered backgrounds.
Poor lighting.
Unwanted shadows.
Improper framing.
Blurry details.
Inconsistent product positioning.
Low-quality backgrounds.
Different lighting conditions across photographs.
Professional product photography can be expensive and difficult to access.
If BazarSetu expects sellers to prepare professional product photographs themselves, it creates another barrier to onboarding.
We therefore need a Product Image Intelligence System that automatically processes ordinary photographs and prepares them for marketplace use.
Primary purpose: Make professional-quality product photography accessible to sellers without requiring photography equipment, editing knowledge or manual image-processing skills.
1.2 What should this system accomplish?
The system must:
Accept ordinary smartphone photographs.
Evaluate their quality.
Detect the product.
Separate the product from its background.
Preserve its original appearance.
Improve lighting and composition.
Create clean marketplace-ready images.
Verify the final output.
Return the image for seller approval.
1.3 Input
The system should accept:
JPEG images.
PNG images.
WebP images.
Multiple photographs of one product.
Different product angles.
Images captured under different lighting conditions.
Initial capture guidance should support approximately 2–5 photographs per product, with suitable views such as front, side, back, top and detail.

SYSTEM 02: COMPUTER VISION AND PRODUCT UNDERSTANDING
2.1 Why do we need to build this system?
An image-processing system can make a photograph look better, but it does not necessarily understand what the photograph contains.
BazarSetu needs to understand the actual product so that it can create meaningful catalogues, identify categories, extract attributes and verify product information.
For example, a photograph of a handmade textile product should provide more information than simply "an object detected."
The system should identify:
What the product appears to be.
Which category it belongs to.
Its visible colour.
Its material characteristics, where identifiable.
Its pattern and design.
Visible text.
Relevant product components.
Primary purpose: Convert product photographs into structured visual information that can support catalogue creation, verification and marketplace categorization.
2.2 Main responsibilities
A. Product classification
Identify:
Product category.
Subcategory.
Product type.
Relevant product family.
Initial category candidates:
Jewellery.
Pottery and terracotta.
Textiles and handloom.
Woodcraft.
Metalcraft.
Home decor.
Paintings.
Bags and accessories.
Food and spices.
Candles.
Toys.
The system should ultimately be category-driven rather than limited to artisan-specific products.
B. Material recognition
Identify visible material characteristics such as:
Wood.
Metal.
Ceramic.
Clay.
Cotton.
Silk.
Leather-like materials.
Glass.
Plastic.
Material recognition must be treated as a prediction.
The model should not claim a material with certainty when visual evidence is insufficient.
For example, a photograph may suggest silk, but actual material composition may require seller confirmation.
C. Colour recognition
Extract:
Primary colour.
Secondary colours.
Dominant colour distribution.
Approximate colour family.
Visible colour variations.
Preserve the distinction between actual product colour and lighting-induced colour changes.
D. Pattern and design recognition
Identify:
Geometric patterns.
Floral patterns.
Embroidery.
Printed designs.
Traditional motifs.
Textures.
Decorative structures.
This will be especially useful for Indian handmade and textile products.
E. Product component detection
Identify visible components.
Examples:
Handles.
Lids.
Clasps.
Straps.
Compartments.
Decorative elements.
Packaging components.
F. OCR and visual text extraction
Read text visible on:
Product packaging.
Labels.
Brand names.
Product tags.
Printed specifications.
MRP and quantity labels where applicable.
Candidate technology: PaddleOCR or another benchmarked OCR system.
Extracted information must be verified before being used in a published catalogue.
G. Multiple-product detection
Identify:
Multiple separate products.
Product sets.
Packaging and product combinations.
Unwanted background objects.
This helps determine whether an image should be accepted, corrected or recaptured.

SYSTEM 03: VOICE INTELLIGENCE
3.1 Why do we need to build this system?
Many artisans and small sellers are more comfortable speaking than typing.
Requiring sellers to write product titles, descriptions, dimensions, materials and manufacturing details creates friction.
BazarSetu should allow sellers to explain their products naturally in their preferred language.
For example:
"This is a handmade wooden box. It takes two days to make. The wood is locally sourced."
The system should understand the description and convert it into structured product information.
Primary purpose: Enable voice-based product cataloguing without requiring sellers to type complex product information or understand English.
3.2 Supported languages
Initial priorities:
Marathi.
Hindi.
English.
Future language expansion:
Gujarati.
Bengali.
Tamil.

Kannada.

Other Indian languages.
Language support must be evaluated independently. We should not assume that performance in Hindi guarantees performance in regional dialects.

Stage E: Product information extraction

Stage F: Missing-information detection

Stage G: Seller confirmation

SYSTEM 04: MULTIMODAL INTELLIGENCE
4.1 Why do we need to build this system?
Image intelligence and voice intelligence provide different types of information.
A photograph may reveal a product's visible colour, shape and packaging, but it may not reveal its weight, production time or manufacturing process.
A seller's voice may provide the material, product name and dimensions, but may not describe every visible feature accurately.
Using only one source creates incomplete product information.
We therefore need a system that combines visual information with seller-provided voice information.
Primary purpose: Create a more complete and accurate understanding of a product by combining image evidence and seller statements.
4.2 Core capabilities
A. Image and voice fusion
Combine:
Product classification.
Visible attributes.
OCR results.
Speech transcription.
Seller-provided product details.
B. Cross-verification
Compare image observations against voice statements.
Example:
Seller says: "This is a wooden jewellery box."
Image model detects: A box-like product with visible wooden texture.
The system can identify consistency between the sources.
C. Contradiction detection
Example:
Seller says: "This is a red product."
Image analysis identifies a predominantly blue product.
The system should flag the conflict and request clarification rather than automatically choosing one source.
D. Source-aware attributes
Each attribute must retain its source.
Possible sources:
Seller voice.
Visual observation.
OCR.
Deterministic calculation.
External verified data.
Human confirmation.
E. Missing-information detection
Identify information that cannot be established from either input.
Ask the seller for clarification only when necessary.
F. Structured product understanding
Combine the information into a unified product record.
4.3 Example workflow
Input:
Image of a packaged spice product.
Voice description: "This is Garam Masala, 200 grams."
Vision system:
Reads visible brand text.
Identifies packaging.
Extracts visible label information.
Voice system:
Identifies product name.
Extracts quantity.
Multimodal system:
Combines the sources.
Identifies contradictions.
Records confidence and provenance.
Requests confirmation for uncertain fields.
4.4 Output
A unified product understanding object.
Example:
{
  "product_name": {
    "value": "Garam Masala",
    "source": "seller_voice"
  },
  "quantity": {
    "value": 200,
    "unit": "grams",
    "source": "seller_voice"
  },
  "brand": {
    "value": "Extracted visible brand",
    "source": "image_ocr",
    "requires_confirmation": true
  },
  "verification_status": "pending_seller_review"
}
4.5 Technical approach
Use:
Vision models.
Speech recognition.
LLM-based structured extraction.
Source tracking.
Deterministic comparison rules.
Confidence calibration.
The system must not assume that a language model's agreement with itself constitutes independent verification.
4.6 Evaluation
Cross-modal attribute accuracy.
Contradiction detection recall.
False conflict rate.
Missing-information detection.
Source attribution accuracy.
Seller correction rate.
Structured output validity.
4.7 Expected outcome
BazarSetu should understand a product using both what the seller says and what the photograph shows, while clearly distinguishing facts from predictions.

SYSTEM 05: PRODUCT CATALOGUE INTELLIGENCE
5.1 Why do we need to build this system?
After understanding a product, BazarSetu still needs to convert that information into a proper marketplace listing.
Most marketplaces require structured information such as:
Product title.
Description.
Category.
Subcategory.
Material.
Dimensions.
Colour.
Weight.
Quantity.
Product variants.
Selling price.
Required regulatory information.
Manually filling these fields is time-consuming and creates barriers for small sellers.
Primary purpose: Convert verified product information into complete, consistent and marketplace-compatible product catalogues with minimal manual effort.
5.2 Responsibilities
A. Product title generation
Generate:
Clear product titles.
Search-friendly titles.
Category-appropriate wording.
Multilingual titles where needed.
Avoid unsupported claims and keyword stuffing.
B. Product description generation
Generate descriptions using:
Seller statements.
Verified visual attributes.
Product category.
Confirmed materials.
Manufacturing details.
Intended use, when supported.
Do not invent certifications, origins, material composition or product benefits.
C. Attribute normalization
Convert unstructured information into standard fields.
Examples:
"Half kilo" → 500 grams.
"Two feet" → standardized length representation.
Colour variations → controlled vocabulary.
Retain original seller statements when normalization could introduce ambiguity.
D. Product variant identification
Identify possible variants such as:
Size.
Colour.
Weight.
Quantity.
Design.
Do not automatically create variants when the evidence is insufficient.
E. Category mapping
Map products to:
BazarSetu internal categories.
Relevant ONDC categories.
Required category-specific attributes.
ONDC taxonomy must be versioned and validated against the applicable specification.
F. Multilingual catalogue generation
Generate appropriate catalogue content in supported languages.
Preserve product names and culturally specific craft terminology where necessary.
G. Catalogue completeness checks
Identify:
Missing mandatory fields.
Incorrect units.
Inconsistent attributes.
Invalid category assignments.
Unsupported claims.
Missing product specifications.
H. Legal and regulatory field verification
For applicable packaged products, OCR and voice extraction may assist with fields such as MRP, net quantity, manufacturer information and dates.
These fields require seller verification and applicable compliance checks before publication.
5.3 Candidate technologies

Open-weight language models.
Structured output generation.

Translation models.

Attributes.
Variants.

Validation results.
Seller approval status.
5.5 Evaluation
Title relevance.
Description factuality.
Attribute extraction accuracy.
Category mapping accuracy.

SYSTEM 06: PRICING INTELLIGENCE
6.1 Why do we need to build this system?
Many small producers and artisans struggle to calculate the actual cost of producing their products.
They may forget to account for:
Raw materials.
Labour.
Production time.
Packaging.
Electricity.
Transportation.
Other overheads.
This can cause sellers to underprice products and lose money.
At the same time, an excessively high suggested price may make products difficult to sell.
BazarSetu therefore needs a pricing system that helps sellers understand their costs and make informed pricing decisions.
Primary purpose: Protect seller profitability through transparent cost calculations and useful selling-price recommendations.
6.2 Main capabilities
A. Cost collection
Collect:
Raw material cost.
Labour cost.
Production time.
Packaging expenses.
Other overheads.
Additional selling expenses.
Use voice input and simple questions to reduce manual effort.
B. Cost calculation
A basic cost model:
Total Production Cost = Material Cost + Labour Cost + Packaging Cost + Overheads
C. Minimum sustainable price
Calculate the minimum price required to cover the relevant costs.
D. Selling-price recommendation
Suggest a selling-price range using:
Production cost.
Seller's desired margin.
Comparable market prices, when reliable.
Product characteristics.
Applicable selling expenses.
E. Margin calculation
Allow sellers to understand the difference between:
Cost.
Selling price.
Gross profit.
Margin percentage.
F. Bulk pricing
Support quantity-based price calculations.
Example:
1 unit: ₹500.
10+ units: ₹450.
50+ units: ₹400.
The example is illustrative.
G. Seller-controlled pricing
The system must provide recommendations, not autonomously decide the final selling price.
The seller retains final control.
6.3 Technical approach
Pricing should primarily be a deterministic calculation engine, not an LLM-generated answer.
Use:
Structured cost inputs.
Configurable calculation rules.
Market data where available.
Statistical models where justified.
Versioned pricing assumptions.
An ML-based price recommendation model can be considered after sufficient reliable transaction and market data exists.
6.4 Output
Estimated production cost.
Cost breakdown.
Minimum sustainable price.
Suggested selling-price range.
Expected profit.
Expected margin.
Bulk price suggestions.
Calculation assumptions.
6.5 Evaluation
Arithmetic correctness.
Cost estimation error.
Recommendation usefulness.
Seller acceptance rate.
Price correction rate.
Difference between predicted and realized selling outcomes, when sufficient data exists.
6.6 Expected outcome
Sellers should understand their costs and make better pricing decisions without losing control over their own prices.

SYSTEM 07: PRODUCT VERIFICATION AND INTEGRITY
7.1 Why do we need to build this system?
AI-generated product images and descriptions can look convincing while containing incorrect information.
Examples:
A generated image changes the product's colour.
A segmentation model removes part of the product.
A description claims a material that the product does not contain.
The seller says one quantity while packaging shows another.
A generated catalogue includes unsupported product specifications.
Such mistakes can damage buyer trust, increase returns and create marketplace compliance problems.
BazarSetu needs a dedicated verification system to prevent these problems.
Primary purpose: Ensure that marketplace images and product information accurately represent the actual product and have been approved by the seller.
7.2 Main capabilities
A. Image fidelity verification
Compare:
Original photograph.
Product mask.
Processed image.
Final marketplace image.
Evaluate:
Shape preservation.
Colour preservation.
Texture preservation.
Missing regions.
Added regions.
Product boundary changes.
B. Attribute verification
Compare product attributes against their sources.
Examples:
Colour from image versus seller description.
Quantity from voice versus package label.
Material from seller statement versus visual prediction.
C. Duplicate product detection
Identify possible duplicate product photographs and listings.
Use image embeddings, similarity methods and catalogue metadata.
D. Product consistency
Ensure:
Images belong to the same product.
Descriptions match the photographs.
Variants are correctly represented.
Product information is consistent across listing fields.
E. Confidence-based review
Possible outcomes:
Approved automatically under validated rules.
Seller confirmation required.
Manual review required.
Processing rejected.
Automatic approval must be restricted to cases where the relevant quality and verification requirements have been demonstrated to work reliably.
F. Suspicious listing detection
Flag:
Potentially misleading product descriptions.
Inconsistent packaging information.
Suspicious image alterations.
Reused images.
Unsupported claims.
7.3 Technical approach
Combine:
Computer vision.
Image similarity.
Per-attribute confidence.
Deterministic business rules.
OCR and voice evidence.
Human review.
Do not rely on one overall AI confidence score to represent every aspect of product correctness.
7.4 Output
A verification record containing:
Image fidelity result.
Attribute consistency results.
Duplicate detection result.
Confidence information.
Warnings.
Required corrections.
Review status.
Audit history.
7.5 Evaluation
False acceptance rate.
False rejection rate.
Product fidelity detection.
Contradiction detection accuracy.
Duplicate detection precision and recall.
Human reviewer agreement.
Seller correction rate.
7.6 Expected outcome
Only accurate, verified and seller-approved product information should reach the marketplace catalogue.

SYSTEM 08: AI ORCHESTRATION AND MODEL MANAGEMENT
8.1 Why do we need to build this system?
BazarSetu will use multiple AI models, APIs and processing services.
For example:
One model may perform segmentation.
Another may identify products.
Another may process voice.
Another may generate structured descriptions.
Our own code may calculate pricing.
If each application module directly integrates with different providers, the architecture becomes difficult to maintain.
Changing providers would require modifications across the application.
We need a central system that controls how AI tasks are assigned, processed, monitored and evaluated.
Primary purpose: Give BazarSetu complete control over its AI workflows while allowing external providers and open-source models to be replaced independently.
8.2 Main components
A. Model Router
Select an appropriate model for each task.
Example:
Segmentation → BiRefNet.
OCR → PaddleOCR.
Speech recognition → Whisper.
Complex visual reasoning → selected vision API.
Description generation → selected LLM.
B. Provider Abstraction
Create standard interfaces for:
OpenAI.
Gemini.
Claude.
Qwen.
Self-hosted models.
Future providers.
Business logic should communicate with BazarSetu interfaces rather than vendor-specific SDKs.
C. Model Registry
Maintain:
Model ID.
Provider.
Version.
Task.
Deployment type.
Evaluation results.
Latency.
Cost.
Production status.
D. Fallback and Retry
If a provider fails:
Retry according to configured policy.
Select an approved alternative where appropriate.
Preserve job state.
Record failure information.
Avoid duplicate processing.
E. Asynchronous Processing
Long-running image and multimodal operations should use background jobs.
Example lifecycle:
UPLOADED → PROCESSING → VALIDATING → COMPLETED
Failure states should provide understandable reasons and safe retry options.
F. Cost and Performance Tracking
Track:
Cost per API call.
Cost per image.
Cost per product listing.
Processing time.
Token usage.
GPU utilization.
Failure rate.
Retry rate.
G. Model Versioning
Every processed asset should retain the model version and processing configuration that produced it.
H. Model Selection Policy
A model must pass BazarSetu benchmarks before production approval.
Selection should consider accuracy, fidelity, latency, cost, licensing and deployment requirements.

8.5 Evaluation

SYSTEM 09: DATA, EVALUATION AND INFRASTRUCTURE
9.1 Why do we need to build this system?
All other AI systems depend on reliable data, storage, infrastructure and evaluation.
Without a structured dataset, BazarSetu cannot properly compare models.
Without versioned storage, it cannot reproduce image-processing results.
Without evaluation, it cannot determine whether an AI improvement actually improves product quality.
Without reliable infrastructure, processing may become slow, expensive or unreliable as seller uploads increase.
Primary purpose: Provide the data foundation, computing infrastructure, storage, evaluation and monitoring needed to operate and continuously improve the entire AI/ML platform.
9.2 Dataset strategy
A. Initial dataset
Proposed starting target: 500–1,000 real product photographs.
Initial categories:
Jewellery.
Pottery and terracotta.
Textiles.
Woodcraft.
Metalcraft.
Home decor.
Paintings.
Expand categories as seller onboarding grows.
B. Capture difficult examples
Do not collect only professional photographs.
Include:
Dark workshop photographs.
Yellow indoor lighting.

3. HOW THE NINE SYSTEMS WORK TOGETHER
Consider a seller uploading a photograph of a handmade product and recording a description.
The complete workflow:
Seller captures product photographs and records voice.
System 09 stores the original assets and creates processing records.
System 08 creates and coordinates processing jobs.
System 01 checks image quality and prepares marketplace images.
System 02 identifies visual product information.
System 03 transcribes and structures the seller's description.
System 04 combines and cross-checks image and voice information.
System 05 generates the structured marketplace catalogue.
System 06 calculates cost and pricing recommendations.
System 07 checks image and catalogue consistency.
Seller reviews and approves the result.
The approved catalogue is made available to the BazarSetu marketplace.
The workflow should support retries, partial failures and seller corrections without losing original data.

4. CROSS-SYSTEM DESIGN REQUIREMENTS
The following rules apply to all nine systems.
4.1 Source attribution
Every important product attribute must identify where it came from.
Examples:
Seller-provided.
Visually detected.
OCR-extracted.
Calculated.
Externally verified.
Human-confirmed.
4.2 Confidence handling
Confidence should be tracked per attribute or prediction, not only as one overall score.
Confidence thresholds must be calibrated using benchmark data.
4.3 Human approval
The seller must be able to review:
Product photographs.
Product name.
Product description.
Product attributes.
Pricing.
Required catalogue declarations.
4.4 Original asset preservation
Original photographs and recordings must never be overwritten by AI-generated outputs.
4.5 Versioning
Every model, processing configuration, dataset and evaluation should be versioned.
4.6 Privacy and security
Restrict access to seller data.
Use secure storage.
Apply appropriate retention policies.
Avoid sending unnecessary personal information to external providers.
Review provider data handling policies.
Maintain auditable access and processing records.
4.7 Failure management
Every system must define:
Input validation.
Failure states.
Retry policies.
Fallback mechanisms.
User-facing error messages.
Recovery procedures.

6. INITIAL MVP PRIORITIES
We should not try to complete every capability before testing with real sellers.
The first technical milestone should be:
One real smartphone photograph → one accurate, professional marketplace-ready product image.
The first image MVP should include:
Image upload.
Quality assessment.
Product detection.
Product segmentation.
Background removal.
Controlled enhancement.
Final image verification.
Seller preview and approval.
After this pipeline works reliably, we expand into product understanding, voice and catalogue intelligence.

7. SUCCESS CRITERIA
The complete BazarSetu AI/ML platform should eventually demonstrate:
Professional product images from ordinary smartphone photographs.
Faithful preservation of actual products.
Reliable product recognition.
Regional-language voice-based catalogue creation.
Accurate image and voice cross-verification.
Structured marketplace-compatible catalogues.
Useful seller-controlled pricing recommendations.
Reliable product integrity verification.
Model-provider independence.
Measurable processing costs and latency.
Continuous improvement from real seller data.
Numerical targets for accuracy, latency, cost and approval rates will be established after the initial benchmark and pilot.

8. FINAL ARCHITECTURE PRINCIPLE
BazarSetu should not become a collection of unrelated AI API integrations.
It should become a commerce-specific product intelligence platform.
Its long-term advantage should come from:
Real Indian product datasets.
Product-specific computer vision.
Regional-language seller intelligence.
Multimodal product understanding.
Reliable marketplace image processing.
Product verification and evaluation.
Proprietary orchestration and workflow logic.
Continuous learning from seller corrections and real marketplace outcomes.
The objective is not to build the largest AI model. The objective is to build the most useful, reliable and economically sustainable AI system for bringing real-world products into digital commerce.
